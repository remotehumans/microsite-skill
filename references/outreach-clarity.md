# Outreach clarity: the page is a sales page, not an audit trail

Read before writing any sprint page, prospect card or campaign summary. Added after a campaign produced 80+ pages its sender called unusable: every sentence hedged, the sprint shrunk to "testing one preparation step", invented reference codes (E1, S2, C3), "check" 25–40 times per page, and gallery cards that described the page instead of the business.

## The test

A busy owner reads the page on their phone for 30 seconds. They must be able to say:

1. "They understand what we do and what we're trying to do."
2. "They've spotted something real about us right now."
3. "The sprint would give us X." (an outcome in time, money or growth)
4. "It costs about £Y, takes 2–4 weeks, and I can book a call."

If any of the four fails, the page fails, whatever the evidence ledger says.

## Two layers, kept apart

- **Research layer** (private): evidence, dates, unknowns, counter-evidence, financials, scores. Keep it rigorous. It never appears on the page in ledger form.
- **Page layer** (buyer-facing): confident, warm, plain. State public facts plainly with a link. Mark a guess once, in a friendly way: "From the outside, it looks like…". Never hedge every sentence. One footer line covers independence and illustration.

Rigour lives in the research. Clarity lives on the page. Honesty rules still apply: no invented results, quotes or figures; illustrative numbers say "for example".

## Lead with revenue

Revenue is the first outcome on every page and card; cost and time come after, framed as time for selling and serving.

- Hero names a revenue outcome: "Win more of the quotes you already get", "Fill more appointments. Keep members longer."
- "What we'd look at first" ranks revenue levers first: enquiry speed and quote turnaround (win rate), enquiry-to-booking conversion, rebooking, retention and memberships, upsell, capacity to take more clients without hiring. Admin savings last.
- Break-even in sales units from their own published prices or values: "One extra £9,000 order covers it", "Four memberships at your £450 a month". Never salary saved.
- Card "opportunity" leads with the revenue line.
- Show the lever and the break-even. Never promise or invent an uplift figure.

## Page copy contract (sprint mode)

Outline, each section ending on the question the next answers, price last:

1. **Hero.** Their goal in their words plus the outcome. "Grow orders without growing the admin." One line saying what the sprint is. CTA.
2. **What we noticed.** Three short, linked facts about them now (an announcement, a hire, a growth step, a stated priority). Name the person if they said it publicly.
3. **Where the time and money probably go.** 3–5 workflows the sprint would look at first, each with a plain outcome: "Quotes out in hours, not days", "Order admin that doesn't need another hire". Labelled "what we'd look at first".
4. **One example on their own material.** A before/after using a real public document or process of theirs. Real names of their products, services and teams. Never invented codes or "fictional bundles".
5. **What you get.** The report: interviews, workflows mapped, process maps, ranked opportunities, roadmap, walkthrough. Show the flip-book.
6. **Proof.** Results from work like this.
7. **The sprint.** 2–4 weeks, four steps, `{{PRICE:sprint}}`, one break-even line from their numbers, free 30-minute call.

The sprint covers a team or the whole business and ends in a roadmap of opportunities. Never shrink it to "testing one small step".

## Writing rules

- Reading age about 10: short sentences (under 20 words), common words, active voice, "you" and "your team".
- Body under 1,200 words.
- Banned on buyer pages: "preparation", "bundle", "fictional", "currentness", "would test", "source-and-question", invented reference codes, lists of decisions "people retain", repeated disclaimers, more than five uses of "check".
- Sell the state: relief, control, room to grow, pride in the work. Ground each feeling in one concrete situation.
- Plus the existing voice rules in `SKILL.md` (British English, no em dashes, no "not X, Y").

## Prospect card contract (gallery, list, CRM)

Each card answers the provider's five questions in plain words, about the business, never about the page:

| Field | Example (Ashby & Rowe, the fictional sample) |
|---|---|
| What they do | Accountancy firm in York and Leeds. About 30 staff, growing its advisory work. |
| Can they pay | £3.4m turnover, £610k operating profit (FY2026 accounts). |
| Why now (linked, dated) | Opened a second office in Leeds in May 2026, and paused a bank-feed trial after mismatched payments (their blog, 14 Jul 2026). |
| The opportunity | Month-end takes about 30% of team time across two offices. A sprint maps it and shows where AI frees partners for advisory work. |
| Who to contact | Helen Ashby, Managing Partner (LinkedIn), who signs off spend. Tom Rowe, Operations Manager, who runs month-end. hello@example.com |
| Opening line | "Congratulations on the Leeds office. I made a short page on how your team could get month-end done sooner:" + link |

Signal chip: the trigger in three to five words ("Hiring for order admin", "New MD, growth plan"). Never "check if the page could help".

## Review gate

Light Claude copy reviews judge the four-part 30-second test and the writing rules above, and return the fixed hero, the four answers in one line each, and any banned words found. A page that is accurate but unclear fails.
